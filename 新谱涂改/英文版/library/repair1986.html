Hall repair note — one 1986 item

Clip　Twp-86-tileNot in the genealogy cabinet

Autumn 1986, the hall changed tiles. West-2 eldest handled a loan of eight hundred. The handling book has a name. Has a mark. After the man left, the eight hundred was not repaid. East branch later kept that sum in the mouth. Heard twice in the teahouse.

The clerk copying stops here. A village loan proves there was an account then. It does not prove the man was adopted-out. Adopted-out needs another paper, a named family, a hall-checked copy. Writing a debt as adopted-out is teahouse talk. Not holdings talk. You Shuang put this item in the town-papers clip so nobody would take it as a book proof.

This note does not enter the genealogy. To see a book line, go back to the 1998 copy. How the east branch stirs the eight hundred into a cell, that is downstairs talk. Special collections does not take it. Eight hundred can explain who has a heat in the chest. It cannot explain the year on an attachment.

Someone asks: should a man who owes money hold a cell. Library answer: a cell is a lineage place. An account is another book in the hall. Do not staple the two into one.
