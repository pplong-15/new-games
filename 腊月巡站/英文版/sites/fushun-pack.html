My mother’s side keeps nian-si. Bundle packed for nian-si night: two jin barley sugar in a tin, one pair paper horses folded, new Kitchen God print on top so it won’t damp. Twenty-eight. Red pen on the tag. You can see it from the market mouth. Some northern people take the print down on the twenty-third. That’s their business. Don’t show up the twenty-third morning and crack my tin. Opened sugar I take back I still can’t sell.

Not looking for a fight. Paper horses left overnight go damp. My mother said: send early and nobody receives. You want it down early, your call. Red pen on the box says nian-si. Don’t treat it as loose weight. Phone dead, bang the door. Old Jia’s ears are sharp.

12th lunar month, 22nd night — added
Eleven paper-horse sets left. I saw the subdistrict paper. Not what my mother said. Twelve years married. Stove facing I couldn’t change. The day I can’t change either. Returns after nian-si is over. I settle accounts then.
