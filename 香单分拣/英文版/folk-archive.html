Ancestral seat and solitary souls, split tables (archive excerpt)

No.: Folk-07Copied: old in-house card

The old card writes: mid-seventh-month, those with heirs enter the ancestral niche; the unclaimed open a separate table. Two tables of incense fire are not to be mixed. Mixed, the shrine-hall account and the association account will not match.

The excerpt explains a habit, not the one line in this shop's iron cabinet. Which Meiwan house is called what — the archive has no private account for tonight.

Some read the habit as "taking a solitary soul into the house accumulates merit." The card does not write that sentence. The card only writes split tables. This excerpt cannot approve niche entry, and it cannot stand as a procedure for opening a ghost gate.

Archivist note: present-day city-management notices, temple pages, and hometown-association notices are newer than this card. The card is used to explain "why tables are split." It is not used to fill an applicant's fields tonight.

The photocopy has an archive stamp. The stamp only proves a copy was made. It does not prove which Meiwan house should set what tonight.

The archive catalog files this under seasonal custom, not under genealogy. People looking for a private Ji name will come up empty in the catalog.
