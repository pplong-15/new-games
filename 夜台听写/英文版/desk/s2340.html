A horn far off. Woman’s voice through a receiver. Wind hard.
“It’s Jiping. I’m off the bus. I’ll take the sixth seven-day. Touqi isn’t mine.”
“Don’t write me as tonight’s paying branch.”
[Someone in the hall: Come if you’re coming. Don’t fight names yet.]

She reports her own name. She also reports the period she will take. Married-out daughters often carry the sixth seven-day. The obit desk has an old piece that puts days and branches on one page. You can match it. Her name appeared in the 22:31 overlay. That was bleed from the reading.

At 23:40 she is still on a platform. Do not tick her beside the altar on the main stretch. First pass I ticked her. Looked at the coach page and took it off. Last arrival is written dead in the coach folder. If the slip on the bus is still there, the sixth-seven packet will be a separate line.

The hall answer tells her not to fight names yet. The fight about names is the transcript and the mimeograph, not this minute she steps down. Wind is hard, like standing under the tin shelter at a request stop. Shelter echo is not hall-beam echo.
