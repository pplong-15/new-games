Correspondent’s old copy: sevens and branches

2011 archive　comparison only

Common writing in this town: the filial son’s branch hosts touqi; a married-out daughter often carries the sixth seven-day; the fourth seven-day is sometimes friends. Miss a period, miss a branch, miss a day, and what you can see is a gift list that will not match, and neighbors pointing. Not a returned-soul method that must be walked step by step.

First-seven money and host of record are not automatically the same word. Which branch’s envelope took the money: look at the book. Who was written as the person running the floor: look at the village sheet. If the two twist, night shift can only mark a recommendation.

If a married-out daughter is still on the road, do not write her as the touqi paying branch. She takes the sixth seven-day. That is another period.

The old copy ends: counting days differs by place. Hangzhou has a “knock the sixth” talk. Fuzhou’s sevens are not the same. Shuangqiao counts from the sixth night. That is on the village calendar page.

The correspondent that year copied “who pays, who hosts the offering” very dead. What is dead is a name-share. It is not permission for you to change someone else’s envelope.
